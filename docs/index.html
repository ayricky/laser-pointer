Made to stay out of the way.

Everything the demo above does is the real app's code, ported to the browser. Here's what it's built around.

Fades by itself

Never clean up

Point, circle, underline. The trail tapers and disappears a second later, so the screen is always ready for the next point.

Keep strokes

Or keep it

Switch to the Pen, or press Alt+K, and annotations stay put — even with the laser off and clicks going through — until you clear them.

Straight lines & shapes

Neat without trying

Hold Alt for a perfectly straight line. Rough circles, boxes, triangles and arrows settle into clean ones; you choose how eagerly.

Eight cursors

A pointer that feels alive

A soft glow, rings, crosshairs, brackets and more, in your laser colour. Each switches on and reacts to a press in its own way.

Clicks go through

Keep driving the app

Right-click clicks the app underneath; the scroll wheels, middle and side buttons still reach it. Your slide clicker keeps working.

Light

Half a megabyte, zero fuss

Native Rust and Direct2D. It uses no CPU while nothing moves, follows every monitor's scaling, and sits in the tray until you need it.

Wherever you're showing something.

It draws over anything on Windows. Pick one to see it in the demo.

Keyboard first.

Every shortcut can be changed or turned off in the settings. Keys like Esc are only taken while the laser is on, so they never get in your way.

Questions

Does it work with PowerPoint, Teams, Zoom or my browser?

Yes. It draws on top of whatever is on screen, so it works with any app, including in a shared screen. Right-click passes a click through to the app underneath, so you can keep advancing slides. The one exception is a game in exclusive fullscreen, which takes the whole display for itself; in borderless or windowed mode it works.

Does it send anything anywhere?

No. It makes no network connections and collects nothing. Settings stay on your PC. See the privacy policy.

Why pay if it's open source?

For the signed, auto-updating Store version, and to support its development. If you'd rather build it yourself, every feature is there for free.

Which Windows versions?

Windows 10 (version 1809 or later) and Windows 11, on any number of monitors with any scaling.
